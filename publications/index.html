Design of a Low Phase Noise Quadrature DCO Using Dual Superharmonic Injection in 55nm CMOS for Ka-Band Applications

Devesh Bhaskaran, Shashidhar Tantry

2025 IEEE Asia Pacific Conference on Circuits and Systems (APCCAS) doi ↗

This paper presents a novel approach to mm-W quadrature differentially controlled dual superharmonic injected digitally controlled oscillator (QDCO). Dual-harmonic injection is adopted to reduce phase noise and improve spectral efficiency. The proposed oscillator supports a frequency range from 26.2 GHz to 32.5 GHz. The simulated results show phase noise of −125.38 dBc/Hz at 1 MHz offset and a jitter of 18.23 fs, with each oscillator core consuming only 5.96 mW on a 1.2 V supply. These results confirm the QDCO's superior phase noise performance and frequency stability for advanced wireless and radar applications requiring accurate timing and signal integrity.

Performance Analysis of LC Tank VCO-Based ADC and Current Starved VCO-Based ADC

Devesh Bhaskaran et al.

2025 IEEE North Karnataka Subsection Flagship International Conference (NKCon) doi ↗

This paper presents a study that explores the performance of a voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO) based analog-to-digital converter (ADC) using an LC tank oscillator, a design approach that diverges from the commonly used current-starved or ring oscillator-based VCOs. The unique advantages and challenges of using an LC tank VCO in ADC applications are highlighted. A comparative analysis between the LC tank and the current-starved VCO-ADC is presented, focusing on key performance metrics including accuracy, spectral purity, power consumption, jitter, and phase noise. Simulations were conducted using Cadence Virtuoso with a 180-nm CMOS process.

Exploring FMU-NET: A Comprehensive Study on Person Identification through Novel Semantic Segmentation Techniques

Devesh Bhaskaran et al.

2023 IEEE International Conference on Evolutionary Algorithms and Soft Computing Techniques (EASCT) doi ↗

The paper proposes novel face semantic segmentation for person identification using FMU-Net, a model that amalgamates components from Residual U-Net and Mobile U-Net architectures to extract precise facial features from newly devised annotated masks. Semantic maps serve as the foundation for training, and test semantic maps are subsequently evaluated across various neural network architectures. A classification layer is integrated with the segmentation model for combined segmentation and identity recognition, evaluated through metrics such as the F1 score. FMU-Net surpasses conventional counterparts while improving time efficiency and reducing model complexity through novel annotated semantic masks.
